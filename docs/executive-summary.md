# Executive summary

## Overview

A collaborative corporate OSINT assessment was conducted to understand what information about an organization could be found through open sources and how that information might contribute to security exposure.

The source report describes a passive investigation and records 14 findings across technical, social, organizational and documentary areas.

## Findings distribution

| Severity | Count |
|---|---:|
| Critical | 4 |
| High | 4 |
| Medium | 4 |
| Low | 2 |
| **Total** | **14** |

## Main themes

The assessment identified broad areas requiring defensive attention:

- Externally visible infrastructure and administrative surfaces.
- Email and authentication configuration.
- Auxiliary or legacy environments.
- Domain, DNS and certificate hygiene.
- Public organizational information that could support targeted social engineering.
- Metadata present in publicly accessible documents.

## Interpretation

The presence of an OSINT finding does not, by itself, demonstrate that a system was compromised or that a vulnerability was successfully exploited. The source report did not establish a successful intrusion. Findings should be validated by the responsible technical owners before remediation decisions are finalized.

## Limitations

Open-source information changes over time. Some observations may require direct validation by the asset owner, and third-party datasets may contain inaccuracies or false positives. This public summary intentionally excludes target-specific evidence.
